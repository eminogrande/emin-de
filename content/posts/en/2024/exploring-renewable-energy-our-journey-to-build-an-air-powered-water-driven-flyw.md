## Introduction

Imagine a system that uses air and water to create movement — a low-cost, renewable way to generate energy. That’s the idea we’re working on, right here in Zanzibar. Our goal is to build a simple prototype that shows how we could use everyday elements — air, water, and a few basic parts — to create sustainable energy.

This project is still in its early stages, but with some creative engineering, we believe it could lead to practical applications. Our prototype aims to harness the natural buoyancy of air in water to turn a flywheel, potentially generating enough power for small tasks. This article walks you through what we’re doing, how it works, and how you can help make it better.

## How It Works

In simple terms, the idea revolves around a large water tank, a small air pump, and a lightweight flywheel with cups attached to its edge. Here’s a breakdown of how we think this system could work:

1. **Air Pump**: At the bottom of a water tank, we have an air pump that sends bubbles up through a tube.
1. **Cups on a Flywheel**: As the air rises, it fills small cups attached to the edge of a flywheel. The trapped air makes each cup buoyant, lifting it and causing the flywheel to rotate.
1. **Energy Generation**: The rotating flywheel is connected to a generator. In theory, as the flywheel turns, it could produce enough power to light a small LED or charge a battery.

The beauty of this design is that it uses natural forces — buoyancy and air pressure — to drive movement without relying on fossil fuels.

## Why This Matters

Many places around the world, including Zanzibar, face challenges with electricity access. Power can be inconsistent, and traditional energy sources are often expensive and environmentally harmful. This project explores an alternative approach, using basic components and renewable resources to create low-cost, sustainable energy.

If successful, this air-powered, water-driven system could offer a practical energy source for small tasks like garden lights, water pumps, or charging small devices.
