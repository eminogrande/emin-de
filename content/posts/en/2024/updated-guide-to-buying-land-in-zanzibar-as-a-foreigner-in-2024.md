## For Private Investors

Private investors looking to acquire land for personal use, such as building a lodge, have two primary options:

### Option 1: Forming a Company

- **Form a Local Company**: Partner with a local Zanzibari who will hold a majority stake.
- **Purchase Land in the Company’s Name**: This allows the company to obtain the title, which can later be transferred to the foreign partner.
- **Advantages**: This method provides a legal framework for ownership and simplifies potential future sales.

### Option 2: Direct Purchase

- **Buy Land in Your Name**: Foreigners can acquire land directly but must understand the limitations.
- **Title Acquisition**: You can obtain a title under your name; however, this restricts your ability to sell the land later. If you decide to sell, you can only do so to a local Zanzibari.
- **Limitations**: This option is less flexible for future investment or resale.

## Step-by-Step Guide for Private Individuals Purchasing Land

If you are a private individual interested in purchasing land to build a lodge or similar property, follow these steps:

### Step 1: Research and Identify Suitable Locations

- **Target Areas**: Focus on areas popular with tourists or those with potential for development, such as Jambiani, Paje, or Stone Town.
- **Market Trends**: Analyze current market trends and property values to make informed decisions.

### Step 2: Verify Land Availability

- **Consult Local Authorities**: Check with the Zanzibar Investment Promotion Authority (ZIPA) and local land offices to confirm that the land is available for purchase.
- **Land Use Regulations**: Ensure that the intended use (e.g., building a lodge) complies with local zoning laws.

### Step 3: Form a Local Company (if applicable)

- **Company Registration**: Register your company with the Business Registration and Licensing Authority (BRELA) in Zanzibar.
- **Local Partner Agreement**: Draft an agreement with your local partner outlining ownership stakes and responsibilities.

### Step 4: Verify Seller’s Ownership

- **Ownership Confirmation**: Verify that the seller has legitimate ownership of the land through the land registry or by consulting the Sheha (local village leader).

### Step 5: Draft a Sale/Purchase Contract

The contract must include:

- Identification details of both parties
- Description of the property (location, size)
- Purchase price and payment terms
- Signatures from both parties and witnesses

Legal compliance with relevant laws is necessary, and hiring a local lawyer is advisable.

### Step 6: Submit Required Documents for Approval

To register the transaction, submit:

- The original contract and copies
- Confirmation from the Sheha
- Seller’s title or certificate
- Buyer’s identification
- Seller’s tax clearance certificate
- Payment for stamp duty (10% of purchase price) and land transfer tax (1% of purchase price)

Documents should be filed with relevant authorities including:

- **District Commissioner’s Office**
- **Valuation and Taxation Office**
- **Land Registry Office**

### Step 7: Verification and Re-measurement of Land

Government officials will assess the land’s boundaries. This process may take time, so regular follow-up is recommended.

### Step 8: Finalize Land Transfer Process

The final steps include:

- Public notice publication to allow objections
- A period for resolving any objections
- Issuance of a lease agreement (up to 99 years)
- Registration as leaseholder in the land registry

## For Real Estate Developers

For larger-scale investments, such as those by real estate developers, the process is more structured:

### Engaging with ZIPA

- **Investment through ZIPA**: Developers should apply for an **Investment Certificate** from the **Zanzibar Investment Promotion Authority (ZIPA)**.
- **Ownership Structure**: Developers can own 100% of their company and the land leased for up to 99 years.

### Minimum Investment Requirements

- **Real Estate and Hotel Investments**: A minimum capital investment of **$2.5 million** is required.
- **Other Sectors**: For investments outside real estate, the minimum capital requirement is significantly lower at **$300,000**.

### Process Overview

1. **Submit a Concept Note**: Outline your proposed project using the Investment Intention Form available at ZIPA.
1. **Application Fee**: Pay a non-refundable fee of **$200** upon submission of your detailed application.
1. **Approval Process**: Once your project is approved, you will receive an Investment Certificate, which is renewable annually until your capital investment is fully realized.

## Current Trends in Zanzibar’s Real Estate Market

Zanzibar’s real estate market is on an upward trajectory, driven by several key factors:

1. **High Rental Yields**: Zanzibar ranks among the top cities in Africa for rental yields, securing 7th place overall. This high demand for rental properties is fueled by an increasing number of tourists and expatriates seeking vacation homes or long-term rentals.
1. **Growing Tourism Sector**: The island’s thriving tourism industry significantly impacts real estate demand. With millions of visitors each year, there is a consistent need for vacation rentals and hospitality services. This trend creates lucrative opportunities for property investors.
1. **Government Initiatives**: The Zanzibar government has taken proactive steps to foster a supportive environment for real estate investment. Initiatives such as “golden visas” (Class C11 residency) allow individuals who purchase property worth at least $100,000 in ZIPA-approved projects to obtain residency permits for themselves and their immediate family.
1. **Development Projects**: Approximately 50 development projects are currently underway in Zanzibar, reflecting strong investor interest and confidence in the market’s potential. Notable projects include Fumba Town and Blue Amber Resort, which highlight the island’s appeal as a prime destination for luxury living.
1. **Affordability Compared to Other Markets**: Real estate prices in Zanzibar are generally lower than those in more established tourist destinations like Dubai and Mauritius. This affordability attracts investors looking for value and potential appreciation over time.

## Challenges Facing Investors

Despite its promising outlook, Zanzibar’s real estate market does face challenges:

1. **Regulatory Hurdles**: The process of obtaining title deeds and residency permits can be slow due to bureaucratic inefficiencies. Streamlining these processes is essential for attracting more foreign investment.
1. **Market Competition**: Zanzibar competes with other attractive markets such as Mauritius and Seychelles, which offer similar natural beauty but may have more established regulatory frameworks that facilitate easier investment.
1. **Economic Stability Concerns**: While Tanzania has maintained steady economic growth, any fluctuations could impact investor confidence and market dynamics.
