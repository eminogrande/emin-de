# **oWhat time is it? It’s Moscow time. Or, how much Bitcoin you can buy with 1 US dollar**

![](../../../media/what-time-is-it-it-s-moscow-time-or-how-much-bitcoin-you-can-buy-with-1-us-dolla/01-91c783b4.png)

In Bitcoin and crypto circles, **“What time is it? It’s Moscow time.”** is a phrase referring to the **Moscow Time (MSAT)**, which is a shorthand way of expressing **how much Bitcoin you can buy with 1 US dollar**.

**Explanation:**

• **Moscow Time (MSAT) = 1,000,000 ÷ BTC price in USD**

• This gives the number of **satoshis per dollar** (sats/USD).

• It is called “Moscow Time” because, when Bitcoin was around **$10,000**, the exchange rate was close to **10,000 sats per dollar**, which coincidentally looked like Moscow’s timezone (**UTC+3:00** = “1000” on a military clock).

**Why It Matters:**

• The lower the BTC price, the higher the number of **satoshis per dollar** (cheaper sats).

• A rising Bitcoin price leads to a **lower “Moscow Time”** since fewer sats are received per dollar.

• It is used by Bitcoiners as a **unit of measurement** to track Bitcoin’s purchasing power in smaller units.

**Example:**

• If Bitcoin is **$40,000**, then:

**1,000,000 ÷ 40,000 = 25 sats/USD**

(You get **25 sats** for every $1)

• If Bitcoin drops to **$20,000**, then:

**1,000,000 ÷ 20,000 = 50 sats/USD**

(You get **50 sats** per $1, meaning cheaper sats)

**Why Bitcoiners Use It:**

• Encourages a **sats-first mindset** instead of thinking in whole BTC.

• Highlights Bitcoin’s increasing scarcity over time.

• Reinforces **stacking sats** while they are still “cheap.”

So when someone says **“It’s Moscow time!”**, they might be referring to **how many sats you can get per dollar at the moment** — a way to track Bitcoin’s price in satoshis instead of fiat.
